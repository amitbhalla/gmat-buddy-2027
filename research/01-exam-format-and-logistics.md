## 1. Exam structure (GMAT Focus / current GMAT)

Source for all bullets in this block unless tagged: https://www.gmac.com/gmat-other-assessments/about-the-gmat-focus-edition/exam-overview [OFFICIAL]

- Quote: "The shortest GMAT exam ever at 2 hours, 15 minutes, with only 3 sections, 64 questions, and no writing assessment."
- Quantitative Reasoning: 45 minutes, 21 questions, "assesses problem solving skills."
- Verbal Reasoning: 45 minutes, 23 questions, "assesses critical reasoning and reading comprehension skills."
- Data Insights: 45 minutes, 20 questions, "assesses data literacy skills."
- Total: 3 x 45 = 135 minutes (2h15m) of testing time, 64 questions. Optional break time is separate (see below).
- Question Review & Edit: "Candidates can bookmark and review as many questions as they want and can change up to three answers per section."
- Section order: "Candidates can take the exam in whatever section order they prefer."
- Free score sends: "After candidates know their score (and not before) they can send it to 5 schools for free."
- Not covered on that GMAC page: calculator, break, adaptive format, unanswered-question handling, whiteboard.

Question types per section [SECONDARY, Magoosh updated June 11, 2026, https://magoosh.com/gmat/gmat-focus-edition-key-changes/]:
- Quant: "21 problem-solving questions in 45 minutes" (Problem Solving only; no Data Sufficiency in Quant).
- Verbal: "Reading Comprehension + Critical Reasoning only", 23 multiple-choice questions.
- Data Insights: 20 questions including Data Sufficiency, Table Analysis, Multi-Source Reasoning, Graphics Interpretation, Two-Part Analysis.
- Answer-change quote: "revisit and modify up to three answers per section as long as there is time remaining."

Calculator [SEARCH-SNIPPET summarising official support page https://support.mba.com/hc/en-us/articles/14076264362907-GMAT-Calculator-and-Scratch-Paper-Note-Taking-Policies (403 to fetch tool)]: on-screen calculator built into Data Insights only; no calculator in Quant or Verbal. Magoosh confirms: "This is the one section that allows the use of a calculator on the GMAT."

Scratch/whiteboard:
- Test centre: [SEARCH-SNIPPET of mba.com support] candidates may not bring paper, notebooks, pens or pencils; centre provides a note-taking booklet and marker. [SECONDARY] TTP says noteboards are "not erasable", can request replacement from the administrator, cannot be removed. https://blog.targettestprep.com/gmat-scratch-pad/ (published Nov 11, 2025). test-ninjas says five laminated note pages and two markers https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-test-day-tips-and-checklist (the 5-page count is UNVERIFIED).
- Online: [SEARCH-SNIPPET of mba.com support] no paper/pens/pencils; erasable whiteboard meeting GMAT requirements. [OFFICIAL via search snippet of https://support.mba.com/hc/en-us/articles/50944683740827-GMAT-Online-Exam-Rules-and-Testing-Environment-Requirements] "one erasable whiteboard no larger than 12 x 20 inc[hes]" (rest not readable). TTP: up to 4 dry-erase markers, one eraser, show blank board to camera before exam, before break, before exam ends; on-screen digital whiteboard also available.

Optional break: [SECONDARY] one optional 10-minute break, taken after Section 1 or Section 2 (not both); recommended after Section 2 by that page. https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-test-day-tips-and-checklist . UNVERIFIED against official page.

Computer-adaptive mechanics: [SECONDARY] test-ninjas: "each of the three sections is computer-adaptive"; IRT-based; correct answers raise difficulty of later questions; early questions weigh more; bookmarks unlimited. https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-guessing-strategy-and-penalty . [SEARCH-SNIPPET] GMAC "In-Depth GMAT Presentation 2025" says the computer uses your answer plus earlier responses to choose the next question (PDF unreadable). https://go.gmac.com/hubfs/07.Assessments/GMAT%20Exam/School%20Resources%202025/In-Depth-GMAT-Presentation-2025.pdf . Adaptivity is at question level per the test-ninjas text ("correct answers raise difficulty of later questions"); official confirmation UNVERIFIED.

Unanswered-question penalty: [SECONDARY] No deduction for wrong answers; unanswered questions are penalised. test-ninjas gives formula "30 x (percentage of questions unanswered)" subtracted on the 60-90 section scale, but attributes it to "test-prep literature". UNVERIFIED (not found in official text). https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-guessing-strategy-and-penalty

## 2. Scoring

Scale [SECONDARY, Magoosh June 2026, https://magoosh.com/gmat/gmat-focus-edition-key-changes/]: each section 60-90; total 205-805 in 10-point increments; "Verbal, Quant, and Data Insights are weighted equally."
Formula [SECONDARY, UNVERIFIED]: Total = ((Q + V + DI - 180) x 20/3) + 205 per https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-score-percentiles-explained

## 6. Prep time and score improvement

- GMAC guidance as reported: study "at least six weeks at a consistent pace"; "most GMAT candidates prepare for 60 hours or more." Source: https://www.mim-essay.com/gmat-gre-prep-time-score-improvement-data [SECONDARY, attributing to GMAC; UNVERIFIED].
- GMAC 2020 survey of previous-edition candidates (as reported): median prep 80 hours; below 500 median 50 hours; 700+ median 100 hours. Same source. UNVERIFIED primary.
- Retake gains: GMAC FAQ "an average improvement of approximately 20-30 points" for repeat test-takers (same source; UNVERIFIED).
- test-ninjas (updated 2026, no dated sources): average student 100-170 hours; 30-50 point gain in 50-80 hours; 50-80 points about 180 hours; 100-150 points 240-360 hours; rule of thumb "multiply your score gap by about 3". https://test-ninjas.com/how-long-to-study-for-the-gmat (commercial, treat as estimate).
- Older GMAC-attributed figures in search snippets: 700+ scorers averaged 99 or 114 study hours. UNVERIFIED, conflicting.
- Superscore context: Magoosh says GMAC's own analysis suggests Superscore may be 20-50 points higher than a single-attempt total; methodology unpublished. https://magoosh.com/gmat/gmat-superscore/
