## 1. OFFICIAL CONTENT SPECIFICATION

### 1.1 GMAC exam overview
URL: https://www.gmac.com/gmat-other-assessments/about-the-gmat-focus-edition/exam-overview (fetched)
- Quote: "The shortest GMAT exam ever at 2 hours, 15 minutes, with only 3 sections, 64 questions, and no writing assessment."
- Quantitative Reasoning: 45 min, 21 questions, "assesses problem-solving skills"
- Verbal Reasoning: 45 min, 23 questions, "assesses critical reasoning and reading comprehension skills"
- Data Insights: 45 min, 20 questions, "assesses data literacy skills"
- Question Review & Edit: candidates "can bookmark and review as many questions as they want and can change up to three answers per section."
- Select Section Order: candidates can take sections in any order.
- Free official score report; send score to 5 schools free after seeing score.

### 1.2 GMAC "new exam details" (original 2023 announcement)
URL: https://www.gmac.com/resources/learners/how-to-apply/exams-preparation/new-gmat-exam-details (fetched)
- 64 multiple-choice questions, three sections of 45 min each, ~2h15m total plus an optional 10-minute break. No essay.
- Quant: 21 questions (31 on the old exam). Covers "arithmetic, problem-solving, and elementary algebra". No data sufficiency. No calculator.
- Verbal: 23 questions (36 on old exam). Covers reading comprehension and critical reasoning. No sentence correction.
- Data Insights: 20 questions, "some testing integrated reasoning and others testing data sufficiency". Measures digital and data literacy. Analyses information in "text, graphic, dataset, and table formats". Includes an on-screen calculator. Combines math, data analysis, and verbal reasoning.
- Review & Edit: bookmark during the section; after each section a numbered list of all questions appears with bookmarked ones marked; edit "maximum of three answers per section."

### 1.3 Confirmations (all via secondary/search summaries; official mba.com pages not renderable)
- Search result snippet attributing to GMAC/mba.com: sections are computer-adaptive; any section order; five DI question types: Data Sufficiency, Multi-Source Reasoning, Table Analysis, Graphics Interpretation, Two-Part Analysis. ### 1.4 Quant topic scope (official = arithmetic, problem solving, elementary algebra; no DS in Quant)
All 21 Quant questions are Problem Solving, multiple choice, 5 options. Official topic-level list was not retrievable; see prep-company taxonomy in section 2.1 (TTP).

### 1.5 Verbal scope
- CR + RC only. TTP article: 23 Qs, 45 min, "1 minute 57 seconds" avg; answers must be given in order; "You can't skip questions and still reach the review screen." (https://blog.targettestprep.com/gmat-focus-edition-verbal-section/)
- GMAC-style description (via TTP https://blog.targettestprep.com/gmat-verbal-question-types/): CR about 9-10 Qs, passages typically under 100 words; RC about 13-14 Qs, usually 3-4 per passage, passages up to 350 words. RC question types listed: "identify the main idea of a passage"; "draw inferences based on statements in a passage"; "understand the meaning and purpose of specific details in a passage"; "understand how a passage is organized"; "identify the author's tone in the passage".
- Note: other sources say RC is ~9-10 Qs over 3-4 passages (Test Ninjas) and CR 9-11; GMAC publishes no fixed split.

### 1.6 Data Insights scope
Five types (e-GMAT estimated share, from https://e-gmat.com/blogs/gmat-data-insights/): DS 20-40%; GI 20-30%; TA 10-20%; TPA 10-20%; MSR 10-20%. DI counts for one-third of the total score. Scored on 60-90 per section (Physics Wallah summary https://www.pw.live/mba/exams/gmat-di).

## 2. PREP-COMPANY TAXONOMIES

### 2.1 Target Test Prep Quant syllabus (19 listed topics; article says "20 major")
URL: https://blog.targettestprep.com/gmat-focus-math-syllabus/ (subtopics as summarised by tool)
1. Basic Arithmetic: fractions, decimals, estimation, order of operations, factorials
2. Linear Equations: one and two variables, systems (substitution/combination), expressing one variable in terms of another
3. Quadratic Equations: expanding/factoring, quadratic identities, zero-product property, equations with fractional expressions
4. Exponents and Roots: operations, rationalizing denominators, perfect/non-perfect roots, fractional and negative exponents, exponential equations
5. Number Properties: even/odd, positive/negative, primes, LCM/GCF, remainders, divisibility, evenly spaced sets, units-digit patterns
6. Inequalities: add/subtract, multiply by negatives, compound
7. Absolute Value: basics, equations, two absolute values equal
8. General Word Problems: money, age, mixture, consecutive integers; business (salary, simple and compound interest, profit/loss); linear vs exponential growth
9. Rates: basic, average, converging/diverging, catch-up, catch-up-and-pass, round trip
10. Work: basic, combined workers, opposing workers
11. Unit Conversions (incl. squared/cubed units)
12. Ratios and Proportions: two/three-part, ratio multiplier
13. Percents: basic, percent of, percent less/greater, percent change
14. Overlapping Sets: set matrix, Venn
15. Statistics: mean, median, mode, weighted average, evenly spaced sets, range, standard deviation
16. Combinations and Permutations: formulas, counting principle, restricted/unrestricted, indistinguishable, circular, code creation
17. Probability: basic rules, mutually exclusive/addition rule, independent events/multiplication rule
18. Coordinate Geometry: axes, slope/y-intercept, parallel/perpendicular, distance/midpoint, circles, graphing inequalities
19. Functions and Sequences: notation, domain/range, compound functions, graphing, function word problems, arithmetic and geometric sequences
- TTP statement on geometry: coordinate geometry is included in TTP's syllabus; non-coordinate geometry (triangles, circles area, solids etc.) "no longer tested". Coordinate geometry status in the real test is not confirmed by GMAC text I could retrieve (GMAC says arithmetic + elementary algebra).
- TTP course size (search summary): 52 chapters, 1,500+ lessons, 4,000+ Quant/Verbal/DI problems; tools: study plan, custom tests, error tracker.

### 2.2 e-GMAT
- Quant syllabus page (fetch returned 404): https://e-gmat.com/blogs/gmat-quantitative-reasoning-syllabus (search snippet only; not verified).

"Prethinking" is its flagship CR strategy. Source: https://e-gmat.com/blogs/gmat-critical-reasoning-practice-questions/
- DI details: https://e-gmat.com/blogs/gmat-data-insights/ (see 2.5)

### 2.3 CR subtypes (Test Ninjas table) 
URL: https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-critical-reasoning-question-types
- Strengthen: "Which of the following, if true, most strengthens..." / "...most supports the conclusion". Approach: identify assumption, pick reinforcing answer.
- Weaken: "...most seriously weakens..." / "...casts the most doubt". Identify assumption, pick undercutting answer.
- Assumption: "The argument depends on which of the following assumptions?" / "...relies on the assumption that". Negation test.
- Inference: "Which of the following can be properly inferred?" / "...must be true". Stay close to text.
- Boldface: "The portions in boldface play which of the following roles?" Label roles (conclusion, premise, counter-argument, background); don't judge strength.
- Evaluate: "The answer to which of the following would be most useful to evaluate?" Pick question whose "yes" strengthens and "no" weakens.
- Paradox: "...most helps to explain..." / "...resolve the discrepancy". Find mechanism reconciling the two facts.
- Complete the Argument: no standard stem; choose option following logically from premises.
- Other taxonomies (search summary): Manhattan Prep groups CR into Assumption / Structure / Evidence families (~10 types); Flaw and Method of Reasoning and Parallel Reasoning appear in several lists. Assumption, Weaken, Strengthen are most common (one source). Prepscholar list: https://www.prepscholar.com/gmat/blog/gmat-critical-reasoning-tips

### 2.4 RC question types
- TTP/GMAC-style five: main idea; inference; meaning/purpose of specific details; organization; tone (see 1.5).
- Test Ninjas six types (https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-reading-comprehension-strategies): Main idea / primary purpose; Detail / specific reference; Inference ("Must be a direct logical consequence... not just plausible. Considered the hardest type."); Function ("why a sentence or phrase appears, not what it says"); Logical structure; Author's tone.
- Application questions (abstract a principle and apply to a new situation) are described by a search-summarised guide; not confirmed as a distinct official type in Focus.
- GMAT Club six types article (403 to fetch): https://gmatclub.com/blog/gmat-reading-comprehension-strategies-for-the-6-question-types/ , search summary listed: main idea, detail, inference, out of context, logical structure, author's tone.
- Kaplan passage map: https://www.kaptest.com/study/gmat/using-a-passage-map/

### 2.5 Data Insights formats (e-GMAT + Magoosh)
Sources: https://e-gmat.com/blogs/gmat-data-insights/ ; https://magoosh.com/gmat/data-insights (tool-summarised)
- MSR: split screen; left side has "three clickable cards", view one at a time. Questions: standard five-choice MC or multiple dichotomous choice (each part of three-part question gets two options such as true/false). Other sources say "two or three tabs". Source tabs are text and/or visual data.
- TA: sortable table of numbers; multiple dichotomous choice (true/false per statement, three statements). e-GMAT: "Assessing the capacity to extract relevant information from sortable tables."
- GI: chart or graph (scatter plots, x-y graphs, bar graphs, pie charts, distribution curves); each question has two drop-down menus filling blanks in a sentence.
- TPA: large prompt followed by a question-and-answer table (two columns of options, you pick one answer per part); parts "may be partly or fully related, but they are always interdependent". Math or verbal content. No partial credit.
- DS: question plus two statements; Yes/No and Value kinds.
- Format rules: must answer before moving on, cannot return except via Review & Edit; "no partial credit. Missing one part of a multi-part question scores zero for that question" (Magoosh summary).
- Leland summary (https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/gmat-data-insights): section "does not reward advanced math"; aim ~2.25 min per DI question; MSR "verify each answer against at least two details from different sources"; TPA "Don't treat the parts as independent"; common errors: using irrelevant info, skipping units/labels, overspending on one item.
- e-GMAT strategy: take official mock to find percentile by type; start with weak areas below 30th percentile; "Begin with TA and GI, then move to TPA and MSR"; accuracy targets on hard questions "about 55% for a 70th-percentile target, and about 70% for 90th percentile or higher."

## 3. STRATEGIES AND TIPS

### 3.1 Timing benchmarks
- Quant ~2:08-2:09/Q (Test Ninjas https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-problem-solving-strategies: "Treat 2 minutes as a soft target... If you reach 3 minutes without being close, guess... and move on").
- Verbal ~1:57/Q (TTP). RC passage: short (200-250 words, 3 Qs) ~6 min total with 60-90 s reading; long (300-350 words, 4 Qs) ~8 min with 2-2.5 min reading (Test Ninjas RC).
- DI ~2:15/Q; Leland says ~2.25 min.
- e-GMAT penalty article: "if you're not closer to the answer after about 2.5 minutes, guess and move on."
- Test Ninjas guess triggers (https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-guessing-strategy-and-penalty): ~2:30 Quant; 2:15 RC; 2:00 CR; 2:15 DI. Checkpoints: 30 s keep working; 30-60 s start eliminating; 60-90 s commit and bookmark; 90-120 s guess, bookmark, move on.

### 3.2 Adaptive scoring, unanswered questions
- e-GMAT (https://e-gmat.com/blogs/the-penalty-thats-destroying-your-gmat-score/): penalty for unanswered questions applied after points for correct answers; formula stated "30 * (% of unanswered questions)". Example: DI, 5 wrong + 1 unanswered: 30 x (1/20 = 5%) = 1.5 points, final DI78. Advice: answer every question, guess in final moments, bookmark guesses.
- Test Ninjas: same formula; "one blank out of 23 costs about 1.3 points, and five blanks cost about 6.5 points"; wrong answers no direct deduction but lower later difficulty; IRT based; "Treat the first 5-7 questions of every section as non-negotiable"; in final minute, fill every remaining question with a consistent letter. Page's own claims, not verified against GMAC.
- CONFLICT / UNVERIFIED: a GMAT Club reply claims penalty for one blank is usually 0 citing a GMAC document (not retrieved). Princeton Review (search summary) says the penalty for wrong answers diminishes toward section end. No GMAC document confirming the 30x formula was found. Treat the formula as vendor claim.
- Official mba.com guessing pages: https://www.mba.com/exams-and-exam-prep/gmat-exam-prep/gmat-guessing (not renderable; search summary says guidance is to eliminate obvious wrong options before guessing).
- Elimination odds on 5-choice: 20% / 25% after one / ~33% after two / 50% after three (Test Ninjas).

### 3.3 Review & Edit
Source: https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-question-review-and-edit-feature ; GMAC pages above.
- Screen appears only after the last question and only if time remains.
- Bookmarks unlimited, navigation only; 3 edits per section; "three changes, not three questions"; reverting uses an edit; unused edits don't carry over.
- Early wrong answers lower difficulty path; correcting later does not reset it.
- Suggested flag budget: ~4-6 Quant, 3-5 Verbal, 3-4 DI; leave ~2 minutes so the review screen appears; edit only with a concrete reason.

### 3.4 Section order
- GMAC: any order, six possible. Test Ninjas (https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-section-order-strategy): lead with strongest section by default; sandwich weakest in the middle if scores similar; break "immediately before the section you expect to be hardest"; pick order in prep, commit by third mock. Claims "No statistically significant score differences across orders" from a "GMAC pilot study" (no link; unverified). Quant -> Verbal -> DI is called "the single most commonly recommended order" (unverifiable).

### 3.5 DS method (AD/BCE) and traps
- TTP (https://blog.targettestprep.com/gmat-data-sufficiency-questions/): Scenario 1: if (1) alone sufficient, eliminate B, C, E; check (2): sufficient = D, else A. Scenario 2: if (1) not sufficient, eliminate A and D; (2) sufficient = B; else test together: C if sufficient, E if not.
- CrackVerbal (https://www.crackverbal.com/resources/gmat-focus-data-sufficiency-guide/): SCAN framework (Sort, Convert, Arrange, Navigate); Value Qs need one unique number; Yes/No sufficient only if always the same answer; traps: the C trap, assuming integers, half-deconstructing the stem, mistaking silence for impossibility, solving instead of assessing. Time ~90-150 s on medium; flag after ~30 s if stuck.

### 3.6 Quant tactics (Test Ninjas PS strategies)
- Plugging numbers: use when variables in answers or percents/ratios; base 100 for percents; use small numbers (2, 3, 4, 10), avoid 0, 1, -1; for "must be true", try to break each choice.
- Backsolving: when question asks for specific value and answers are clean numbers; test middle choice first.
- Estimation: look at answer spread first; "The GMAT rewards correct answers, not precision."
- Traps: answering a different quantity, missing unit conversion, sign flip. Write the target quantity on scratch paper.

### 3.7 RC and CR tactics
- RC mapping (Test Ninjas): "Read for structure, not detail"; 8-12 words per paragraph on purpose; slow down at transition words; paraphrase technical terms. Traps: extreme language; word-matching (correct answers paraphrase); outside knowledge; scope errors (too narrow/too broad/reversed). TTP advice: "Read for overall understanding, not detail" and "Return to the passage as necessary."
- CR: identify conclusion and premises, prephrase assumption (e-GMAT "Prethinking"); assumption negation test; Evaluate answers as yes/no questions; only one answer correct, "If more than one answer seems surely correct, reconsider"; use common knowledge only.

## 5. STUDY PLAN BEST PRACTICES

### 5.1 Hours (sources, all vendor/estimates; none from GMAC)
- TTP (https://blog.targettestprep.com/how-many-hours-study-for-gmat-focus/): "About 180 hours of studying to achieve a 50- to 80-point increase"; 100-150 point increase about 240-360 hours; "15+ hours per week over 3-6 months". - Test Ninjas: 10-20 hrs/week realistic for working professionals (search summary).
- The user's hint figures (100-150 hrs for 645+; 150-250+ for 705+) were NOT found stated in a Focus-specific source. Princeton Review (via search) equates Focus 645 ~ old 700 and 665 ~ old 720.
- GMAT Club anecdote: 645 after ~6 months, 4-5 h/day, starting at 505 (https://gmatclub.com/forum/from-a-505-to-a-429172.html).

### 5.2 Plan structures
- Test Ninjas 12-week plan (https://www.test-ninjas.com/gmat-three-month-study-plan): Weeks 1-4 diagnostic (Week 1, free Official Starter Kit exam, full timed) + Quant/Verbal fundamentals, DI from Week 3; Weeks 5-8 strategy/pacing, 605-level sets, timed drills; Weeks 9-12 mock-heavy, 705-level questions, dress rehearsal, taper. Hours/week 10-15 baseline, 18-24 intensive. Mocks: Week 4, 6, 8, 10, final Week 11 at least 7 days before test; 2-3 h review within 48 h of each mock; ~2.5 h for the test itself. Error log from Week 2: columns date, question type, topic, reason missed (content gap, careless, time pressure, misread), time spent; review weekly and before each mock. 1-2 rest days/week.
- GMAT Club study plan (https://gmatclub.com/study-plan/): 12-week plan; Focus-specific; retakers use latest score report as baseline. GMAT Club forum post: weight ~40% of time to weakest section; three months enough for fundamentals, systematic practice, multiple mock rounds.
- TTP: linear topical plan; learn topic, practice, easy/medium/hard chapter tests; flashcards for formulas; start with a full-length practice test baseline.
- e-GMAT: free plan assessment tool; take official mock to find percentile by DI type (see 2.5).
- Burnout prevention: rest days, taper in final week (Test Ninjas); no GMAC source.

### 5.3 Research on learning
- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. Psychological Science, 17(3), 249-255. DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x. PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/ ; APS summary https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/test-enhanced-learning . Findings per APS: Experiment 2 testing group read material ~3.4 times and recalled 61% one week later; restudy group read ~14.2 times and recalled 40%. On the 5-minute test restudying did better. Restudiers were more confident but remembered less.
- Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354-380. PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/ ; York U page https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html . Quote: "This review found 839 assessments of distributed practice in 317 experiments located in 184 articles." Finding: optimal gap between sessions increases as the retention interval increases.
- Both papers use verbal/prose recall tasks; applicability to GMAT quant skills is an inference, not stated by authors.

## 6. QUESTION STYLE DETAILS (for writing realistic practice items)

### 6.1 Data Sufficiency standard answer choices
Verbatim as quoted by TTP (https://blog.targettestprep.com/gmat-data-sufficiency-questions/):
- A: "Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient to answer the question asked."
- B: "Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient to answer the question asked."
- C: "BOTH statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are sufficient to answer the question asked, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient to answer the question asked."
- D: "EACH statement ALONE is sufficient to answer the question asked."
- E: "Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient to answer the question asked, and additional data specific to the problem are needed."
CrackVerbal's shortened variant (https://www.crackverbal.com/resources/gmat-focus-data-sufficiency-guide/): A "Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient." / B mirror / C "BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient." / D "EACH statement ALONE is sufficient." / E "Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient." Choice text is identical for every DS question; order is always A-E as above. Note: neither is directly GMAC's page; the long form matches the classic GMAC wording.
Structure: question stem (may include extra information), Statement (1), Statement (2), five fixed choices. Value (one unique number) vs Yes/No (always yes or always no).

### 6.2 Other DI formats
- TPA: one prompt with two linked tasks; table with two columns and a shared set of option rows; select one option per column (both must be correct for credit).
- MSR: 2-3 tabs (text/tables/charts); each question either standard 5-choice MC or three dichotomous (yes/no, true/false) statements.
- TA: sortable table (column sort), three statements each true/false (or yes/no).
- GI: graph or chart plus a sentence with two drop-down blanks, each with a few options.
- PS (Quant): five answer choices, no calculator; Verbal CR: short argument (under ~100 words) + one question; RC: passage up to ~350 words, 3-4 questions.
