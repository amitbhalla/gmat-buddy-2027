# Your Resource Guide

There's a huge amount of GMAT material out there, and most of it is unnecessary. This lesson tells you what's worth having, what's free, what's worth paying for, and what a sensible budget looks like for you. Prices change often and most of the figures below come from prep-company sites and retailers, not from the providers' official pages, so please check current prices before you buy. I haven't personally tested every product, so the verdicts reflect general reputation and what the sources say.

## The golden rule

**Start with official material. Everything else is a supplement.** Only GMAC writes real GMAT questions, and only GMAC's practice exams use the real scoring system. Third-party material can teach well and give extra practice, but its questions are written by other people and its difficulty is calibrated differently.

Also remember that this app already gives you lessons, practice, flashcards, an error log and mocks. You don't need a course on top of it. You may want one if you like video teaching or want extra questions.

## Free resources

**GMAT Club.** A huge forum with a question bank, study plans and score stories. It also offers mock exams, with one free and the others paid. The forum is a great place to read explanations, but be careful: older posts often refer to the old exam, so check the date and the question type.

**Khan Academy.** Free maths lessons and practice from basic arithmetic upward. If fractions, percents or algebra feel shaky, work through the relevant sections in the first phase. It's well suited to rebuilding school-level maths.

**YouTube.** Several prep companies and coaches post free lessons. The quality varies and many videos pre-date the current exam, so look for recent videos about the current format. GMAT Club's and e-GMAT's channels are among those commonly listed. Don't watch endless videos in place of practising questions.

**Reddit and Beat The GMAT.** Communities where people share study plans, test-day stories and score updates. Useful for morale and recent exam experience. Take advice with a pinch of salt.

**Free trials and free mocks from paid providers.** Several offer a free mock or a free trial: for example, one e-GMAT mock is reported free, one GMAT Club mock, one Experts' Global mock. They give you a taste and extra practice at no cost.

## Reading sources for your Reading Comprehension habit

Reading Comprehension passages are academic-style, drawn from business, science, social science and the humanities. The best preparation is reading similar writing regularly, for pleasure, as a habit. Aim for 20 to 30 minutes a day, a few times a week.

| Source | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **The Economist** | Dense, well-organised argument on business, politics and science. Short articles are ideal practice. |
| **Aeon** | Long essays on philosophy, science and culture. Good for abstract, idea-heavy writing. |
| **Scientific American** | Clear science writing, useful for passages on research and methods. |
| **Smithsonian Magazine** | History, science and culture in accessible prose. |
| **The Atlantic** | Long-form essays on society, technology and ideas. |
| **Harvard Business Review** | Business reasoning, closest to the kind of content in Critical Reasoning and Data Insights. |

Some of these sites limit free articles or ask for a subscription, so use what's open to you. Your library or college may offer access.

### How to read for the GMAT

Don't just read. Practise the same moves you'll use in the exam.

1. After each paragraph, say its job in 8 to 12 words (for example: "introduces a theory", "gives an objection").
2. After the article, state the main point in one sentence.
3. Ask: what is the author's attitude, and what would the author most likely agree with?
4. Notice transition words (however, although, therefore, in contrast), because they signal where the argument turns.

Reading the Economist's charts and articles will also help your Data Insights sense of how numbers support claims. Your marketing job already gives you a head start there.

## Tools

- **A flashcard or notes system.** The app has its own flashcards, which is enough. If you also like a separate tool, Anki is a popular option (its price varies by device).
- **A simple timer.** Your phone's stopwatch is enough for timed sets.
- **Data practice.** Public data sites, such as Our World in Data, are good for practising reading charts and tables.
- **A proper computer for mocks.** The official exams are meant to be taken on a computer, so use a laptop or desktop and a mouse.

## Key takeaways

- Start with the free official Starter Kit and Practice Exams 1 and 2, then add the Official Guide 2026-2027.
