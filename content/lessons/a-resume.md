# The One-Page Admissions Resume

Your admissions resume isn't a job resume. It's a one-page summary that helps a reader decide, in about a minute, whether you're worth a longer look. Keep it tight, quantified and honest.

> [!NOTE]
> Many schools specify a format or page limit in their application, and some ask you to fill in structured forms instead. Always follow the school's own instructions first. The advice below is the default for when they don't specify.

## What an admissions resume does differently

| Job resume | Admissions resume |
|---|---|
| Lists responsibilities | Shows results and leadership |
| Tuned to one job description | Tuned to show growth and potential |
| Skills-heavy | Story-heavy |
| Can run two pages | One page, always |

The reader is a committee member who might read hundreds. They want to see, fast: where you studied, what you've achieved, how you've led, and what else you do.

## The structure

1. **Header:** name, city, email, phone, LinkedIn.
2. **Education:** Jesus and Mary College, University of Delhi. Degree, year, and a distinction or rank if you have one. Add class 12 results only if requested.
3. **Work experience:** reverse chronological. Company, role, dates. Three to five bullets per role.
4. **Extracurriculars and community:** two to four items, with roles and dates.
5. **Additional:** languages, certifications, test scores (GMAT once you have it).

Keep it to 10 to 11pt font, clean margins, no photo, no table graphics, no colours beyond black.

> [!TIP]
> Use one simple, standard font. Some application systems strip fancy formatting. Save as PDF unless told otherwise.

## The bullet formula

Every bullet follows the same pattern:

**Action verb + scope + metric (+ how or why).**

- **Action verb:** led, built, launched, redesigned, negotiated, trained, grew, cut.
- **Scope:** budget, audience size, team size, number of markets or products.
- **Metric:** the result, in numbers where possible.

Example: "Launched a three-city festive campaign across 60 retail stores, lifting sales 22% against the previous Diwali."

Check each bullet against four questions:

1. Does it start with a strong verb, not "responsible for"?
2. Is there a number?
3. Is it clear what *you* did?
4. Would a stranger understand it without company jargon?

> [!TRAP]
> Weak openers: "responsible for", "helped with", "assisted in", "worked on". They hide your contribution. Replace them with what you actually did.

## Rewriting typical marketing bullets

| Typical bullet | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| "Responsible for managing company social media accounts" | "Grew LinkedIn followers from 3,500 to 9,200 in nine months through weekly thought-leadership posts; generated 140 qualified leads" |
| "Helped create email newsletters" | "Redesigned the monthly newsletter for 12,000 subscribers, increasing click-through rate from 1.8% to 3.1%" |
| "Worked with the sales team on a product launch" | "Partnered with the sales team to launch a new service in two segments, contributing to INR 80 lakh in first-quarter pipeline" |
| "Managed events" | "Planned a 250-person customer summit with a budget of INR 9 lakh, delivered 10% under budget with a 4.6/5 attendee rating" |
| "Did market research" | "Surveyed 400 customers and synthesised findings into a positioning change that was adopted across three product pages" |
| "Coordinated with agencies" | "Managed two creative agencies on a INR 25 lakh campaign, cutting delivery time by two weeks through a shared tracker" |

These numbers are examples only. Use your own real figures. If you don't know a figure, estimate honestly and say "approximately", then confirm with your manager.

> [!REMEMBER]
> Never exaggerate. Interviewers often start with a bullet and ask three follow-up questions. If you can't defend a number, remove it.

## Showing leadership on a young resume

If you haven't managed people, show other kinds of leadership:

- "Mentored two interns on content creation; both received full-time offers."
- "Initiated a weekly reporting dashboard adopted by the marketing team."
- "Presented campaign results to the leadership team each quarter."

Leadership doesn't need a title. It needs verbs that show you moved people or processes.

## Extracurriculars and community

List them as you would a job: role, organisation, dates, and one result line.

Example: "Volunteer English tutor, Teach for a Better Tomorrow (illustrative name), 2024 to present. Taught weekly classes to 15 students; 11 improved their school scores."

Three items done well beat eight done thinly.

## Education and test scores

Include your GMAT score once you have it, with the date and the section scores if they help. Only list one official score unless a school asks otherwise. Note that the Focus Edition scale runs from 205 to 805.

If your college grades are strong, mention them. If they're modest, don't hide them, but balance with other strengths.

## Common mistakes

- Going over one page.
- Starting bullets with "responsible for".
- Using company jargon that outsiders won't understand.
- Listing duties instead of results.
- Having inconsistent dates and tenses. Use past tense for past roles and present tense for your current one.
- Typos. Read it aloud and ask a friend to read it.

- Responsible for running paid ads
- Created reports for management
- Worked with design team on creatives

- "Managed INR 4 lakh monthly paid-ads budget across Google and Meta; cut cost per lead 31% (INR 900 to INR 620) through audience and creative testing."

Step 3: check that each bullet has a verb, scope and result, and that she can defend each number.

## Key takeaways
- An admissions resume is one page and tells a story of results and leadership.
- Use the formula: action verb, scope, metric.
- Lead each role with your strongest result, and cut duty-style bullets.
- Show leadership through ownership, mentoring and initiative, even without a team.
- Keep extracurriculars to a few, with roles and results.
- Only include numbers you can defend in an interview.
