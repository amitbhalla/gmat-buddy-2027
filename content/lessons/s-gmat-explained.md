# The GMAT in Plain English

Before you study anything, you should know exactly what you're walking into. The current GMAT is shorter and simpler in shape than the exam your seniors may have taken, and once you can picture all 2 hours and 15 minutes of it, a lot of the fear goes away.

## The big picture

The exam has three sections. Each is 45 minutes. In total, that's 64 questions and 2 hours 15 minutes of testing time, plus one optional 10-minute break. There's no essay.

| Section | Questions | Time | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantitative Reasoning | 21 | 45 min | Arithmetic and algebra, solved without a calculator |
| Verbal Reasoning | 23 | 45 min | Critical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension |
| Data Insights | 20 | 45 min | Reading and reasoning with data, with an on-screen calculator |

Average time per question works out to about 2 minutes 9 seconds in Quant, 1 minute 57 seconds in Verbal and 2 minutes 15 seconds in Data Insights. We'll turn those into real checkpoints in the pacing lesson.

Each section is scored from 60 to 90, and your total runs from 205 to 805. All three sections count equally. That's an important point for you: Data Insights is worth just as much as Quant, and it's the section where your marketing experience can give you an edge.

> [!REMEMBER]
> 3 sections, 45 minutes each, 64 questions, 2 h 15 min. Quant 21, Verbal 23, Data Insights 20. All three count equally in your total.

## Quantitative Reasoning: 21 questions

Every Quant question is a **Problem Solving** question: a short problem and five answer choices. You work it out and pick one.

What's in it: arithmetic (fractions, decimals, percents, ratios, exponents, number properties), and algebra (equations, inequalities, quadratics, functions, word problems, rates, statistics, sets, counting and probability). What's not in it: geometry. No triangles, circles or solids. GMAC describes this section as arithmetic, problem solving and elementary algebra. Some prep companies still teach a little coordinate geometry (graphs, slopes). GMAC's own wording doesn't name it, so I'd treat it as low priority and confirm on mba.com if you want to be certain.

You can't use a calculator here. That's why the numbers are friendly, and why the app trains mental maths early.

## Verbal Reasoning: 23 questions

Two question types.

**Critical Reasoning (CR).** A short argument, usually under 100 words, and one question about it. You might be asked to find an assumption, strengthen or weaken the argument, draw a valid inference, explain a puzzle or spot a flaw. Prep companies estimate roughly 9 to 10 CR questions per exam, but GMAC doesn't publish a fixed split.

**Reading Comprehension (RC).** A passage of up to about 350 words, followed by three or four questions. They test main idea, details, inferences, why the author included something, and tone. The passages are about business, science, history and social topics, written in an academic style. Estimates put RC at around 13 or 14 questions, again not an official number.

What's gone: **Sentence Correction.** There are no grammar-and-style questions any more. So you can spend your study time on reasoning and reading instead.

## Data Insights: 20 questions

This is the newest section, and it mixes maths, data reading and verbal logic. There are five question types.

- **Data Sufficiency (DS):** you're given a question and two statements. You don't solve the question. You decide whether the statements give you enough information to solve it. Answer choices are the same five every time.
- **Multi-Source Reasoning (MSR):** two or three tabs of information (emails, tables, charts), with questions that need you to combine them.
- **Table Analysis (TA):** a sortable table of data, and three statements that you mark true or false (or yes or no).
- **Graphics Interpretation (GI):** a chart or graph, then a sentence with two drop-down blanks you complete.
- **Two-Part Analysis (TPA):** a scenario and a small grid. You choose one answer for each of two linked parts, and both must be right to score.

Prep companies estimate the mix as roughly 20 to 40 percent DS, with the other four types sharing the rest. GMAC doesn't publish this split. An on-screen calculator is available in this section only, and it's a basic one.

> [!TIP]
> A lot of Data Insights questions feel like a day at work: reading a table, spotting what matters, ignoring noise. You already do that with campaign reports. This section is where your job trains you for the exam.

## What's NOT on the exam any more

Let's make this list explicit, because older books and videos still teach these things.

- **Geometry** (triangles, circles, polygons, solids, area, volume).
- **Sentence Correction** (grammar and style).
- **The essay** (the old Analytical Writing Assessment).
- **Data Sufficiency in Quant.** DS now lives in Data Insights only.

If a resource spends a chapter on any of these, it was written for the old exam. Skip those chapters. Older Official Guide-style material can be useful for practice questions, but check what each question type is before doing it.

> [!TRAP]
> Don't buy or download a "GMAT" book without checking it covers the current exam. Plenty of cheap second-hand books still teach geometry, Sentence Correction and the essay, and will cost you weeks.

## Calculator, whiteboard and scratch work

**Calculator.** Only in Data Insights, and it's on-screen. In Quant and Verbal you have none.

**Scratch work at a test centre.** You can't bring paper or pens. The centre gives you a note-taking booklet or laminated note boards and a marker. Prep companies report that these aren't always erasable and that you can ask the administrator for a replacement, but you can't take them out. The exact details can vary by centre and policy, so confirm on mba.com.

**Scratch work online.** You use a physical erasable whiteboard that meets GMAC's size rules (the official rule is no larger than 12 x 20 inches) and markers, and you show it to the camera as the proctor asks. There's also an on-screen digital whiteboard option as a backup. We'll cover this in the test-day lesson.

Either way, practise writing neatly and compactly. Your scratch space is smaller than a school notebook, and a messy board causes careless errors.

## Section order and breaks

You choose the order of the three sections at the start. There are six possible orders. There's no "right" one for everyone, and we'll help you pick in a later lesson.

You get one optional 10-minute break. Prep companies say it's taken after the first or second section, and you can't take both. Confirm the current rule on mba.com. Note that the break isn't part of your 2 h 15 min, so using it doesn't cost you testing time.

## How the test adapts

The exam is **adaptive within each section**. In simple terms, the software picks the next question based on how you've been doing. Get questions right and the later ones tend to be harder. Get them wrong and they get easier. Harder questions are worth more to your score, which is why getting stuck on easy ones early is costly. We cover this in depth in the scoring lesson.

Practically, three rules follow.

1. You answer questions in order. You can't skip forward and come back.
2. Once you answer, you can't just go back, except through Review & Edit (see below).
3. Every question needs an answer. Unanswered questions are penalised.

## Review & Edit

At the end of each section, if you have time left, you get a **Review & Edit** screen. During the section you can bookmark questions. After the last question, you see a list of all questions with your bookmarks marked. You can go back and change up to three answers in that section. It's three changes, not three questions, and you can't carry unused edits over to another section. Details and strategy are in the section-order lesson.

## The Superscore

In August 2026 GMAC launched the **GMAT Superscore**. It automatically takes your highest score in each section from across your valid attempts on the current exam, and shows the best combined result. GMAC says it appears in your mba.com account and as an extra data point on official score reports sent to programmes. A prep company reports that you can't hide it when sending scores. Confirm the details on mba.com.

For you, the meaning is simple: a second attempt can only help your section strengths. That's why the plan includes a backup attempt in October 2027.

## Key takeaways

- Three sections of 45 minutes each: Quant 21, Verbal 23, Data Insights 20. That's 64 questions in 2 h 15 min.
- Scores run 60 to 90 per section and 205 to 805 total, with the three sections weighted equally.
- Quant is Problem Solving only, arithmetic and algebra, no calculator, no geometry.
- Verbal is Critical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension only. No Sentence Correction.
- Data Insights has five types (DS, MSR, TA, GI, TPA) and the only calculator.
- You pick the section order, and you get one optional 10-minute break.
- Review & Edit lets you change up to three answers per section if time remains.
- The Superscore combines your best section results across valid attempts.
